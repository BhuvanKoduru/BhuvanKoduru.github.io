Research & Publications

I study the gap between what models represent and what they act on. Audio-language models encode how something is said but lose it before they respond; models that refuse harmful requests in chat mostly stop refusing once they act as computer-use agents. I use mechanistic interpretability, activation steering, and large-scale evaluation to find where these gaps open up, and build agents and benchmarks that account for them.

Heard but Not Heeded: Paralinguistic Information Encoding and Loss in Audio-Language Models
B. Koduru, D. Alharthi, R. Singh, B. Raj
CoLM 2026

A mechanistic analysis of four audio-language models (Whisper-large-v2, Qwen2-Audio, Qwen2.5-Omni, Chroma-4B). All of them strongly encode speaking style in the late audio encoder, but that information consistently degrades before the output; the projector reshapes it rather than discarding it.

RIVET: Robust Idempotent Voice Attribute Editing
D. Alharthi, B. Koduru, R. Singh, B. Raj
INTERSPEECH 2026 oral

Attribute labels in large speech datasets are noisy, which makes voice editors unstable. Training the editor to be idempotent (f(f(x)) = f(x)) acts as an implicit regularizer against label noise, improving edit success and speaker-identity preservation for attributes like age and gender.

Chat-safe is not CUA-safe: Chat Methods May Not Transfer to Computer Use
B. Koduru, J. Yang, A. Li, X. E. Wang
NeurIPS 2026 Workshop Agents in the Wild · poster

Across 8 models and 49 tasks, refusal collapses from 31/47 in chat to 4/47 when the same models act as computer-use agents. Activation steering that raises chat refusal (0.71 → 0.85) leaves agent actions unchanged, so chat-time safety methods can't be assumed to transfer.

Subliminal Prosody Learning: Auxiliary Emotion Supervision Redistributes Affective Representations Across ALM Layers
