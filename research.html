Research & Publications

My research focuses on AI safety, multimodal learning, and understanding how foundation models encode and process information. I work on making AI systems more interpretable, controllable, and aligned with human values.

2026

Heard but Not Heeded: Paralinguistic Information Encoding and Loss in Audio-Language Models
Bhuvan Koduru, Dalaver Alharthi, Rita Singh, Bhiksha Raj
CoLM 2026

We investigate how audio-language models encode and preserve paralinguistic information such as emotion, prosody, and speaker characteristics across their internal representations. Our findings reveal systematic information loss patterns that impact downstream performance.

Subliminal Prosody Learning: Auxiliary Emotion Supervision Redistributes Affective Representations Across ALM Layers
Bhuvan Koduru, Dalaver Alharthi, Rita Singh, Bhiksha Raj
ICML MI 2026 Workshop

Through mechanistic interpretability techniques, we demonstrate how auxiliary emotion supervision reshapes the distribution of affective representations across audio-language model layers, improving emotional understanding without explicit fine-tuning.

RIVET: Robust Idempotent Voice Attribute Editing
Dalaver Alharthi, Bhuvan Koduru, Bhiksha Raj, Rita Singh
INTERSPEECH 2026

We present RIVET, a novel approach for robust and idempotent voice attribute editing that maintains speaker identity while enabling precise control over paralinguistic features.

Computer Use with Evolving Software
Siddharth Agashe, Guillermo Gonzalez-Pumariega, Bhuvan Koduru, Jiachen Yang, Ang Li, Xin Eric Wang
CoLM 2026 Workshop

We advance AI safety by developing evolving harnesses for computer-use agents, enabling safer and more reliable autonomous systems in dynamic software environments.

AmongUs-X: Benchmarking Strategic Deception of LLM Agents via Theory-of-Mind Metrics
Kian Mirakhor*, Bhuvan Koduru*, Apostolos Kontogiannis, Ryan Zabounidis, Katia Sycara, Wonsuk Kim
NeurIPS 2026 Under Review *Equal Contribution

We introduce AmongUs-X, a novel benchmark for evaluating strategic deception and Theory of Mind capabilities in LLM agents through 8,000+ simulated multi-agent games. Our game-theoretic framework reveals fundamental limitations in current LLMs' ability to model others' mental states and engage in strategic deception.

Research Interests

AI Safety & Alignment

Developing methods to make AI systems safer, more controllable, and aligned with human values. This includes work on steering mechanisms, safety interventions, and understanding failure modes in agentic systems.

Multimodal Learning

Understanding how audio-language models encode and process paralinguistic information across modalities. Investigating information flow and representation learning in vision-language models.

Mechanistic Interpretability

Reverse-engineering neural networks to understand their internal representations and computational mechanisms. Using interpretability tools to improve model behavior and safety.

Multi-Agent Systems

Designing and analyzing multi-agent systems with focus on strategic interaction, deception, and Theory of Mind. Building evaluation frameworks for agent capabilities.
