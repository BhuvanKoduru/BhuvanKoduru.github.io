About

I'm a Master's student in AI/NLP at Carnegie Mellon University's Language Technologies Institute, graduating in December 2026. I'm looking for full-time research scientist, research engineer, and machine learning engineer roles starting in 2027.

My research asks where the gap opens between what a model represents and what it acts on. Audio-language models encode how something is said but lose it before they respond (CoLM 2026). Models that refuse harmful requests in chat mostly stop refusing once they act as computer-use agents (NeurIPS 2026 workshop). I work with mechanistic interpretability, activation steering, and large-scale evaluation.

This summer I was a research intern at Simular.ai, working on self-evolving computer-use agents and agent safety. Before that I built AmongUs-X at CMU's Robotics Institute, a benchmark of 8,000+ Among Us games measuring deception and Theory of Mind in 21 LLMs. Before CMU, I built multi-agent systems at Adobe and edge computer-vision models at Nosh Robotics.

Selected publications

  1. encoded vs. said

    Heard but Not Heeded: Paralinguistic Information Encoding and Loss in Audio-Language Models→

    B. Koduru, D. Alharthi, R. Singh, B. Raj

    CoLM 2026

    Speaking style is strongly encoded in the audio encoder of four audio-language models, but degrades before it reaches the output.

    arXiv PDF Poster Project page & audio
  2. chat31/47 agent4/47 refusals

    Chat-safe is not CUA-safe: Chat Methods May Not Transfer to Computer Use

    B. Koduru, J. Yang, A. Li, X. E. Wang

    NeurIPS 2026 WorkshopAgents in the Wild · poster

    Refusal drops from 31/47 in chat to 4/47 when the same models act as computer-use agents; activation steering that raises chat refusal leaves agent actions unchanged.
