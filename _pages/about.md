I'm a Master's student in AI/NLP at Carnegie Mellon University's Language Technologies Institute (graduating Dec 2026), where I work at the intersection of **AI safety**, **multimodal learning**, and **agentic systems**. My research spans from understanding how audio-language models encode paralinguistic information to building safer computer-use agents that can be steered away from unsafe behaviors in real-time.

Currently, I'm a Research Intern at **Simular.ai** (advised by Xin Eric Wang, Jiachen Yang, and Ang Li), where I'm reverse-engineering the internal representations of vision-language models to enable real-time intervention on computer-use agents. Previously at CMU's Robotics Institute, I engineered evaluation harnesses simulating 8,000+ multi-agent games to quantify deception and Theory of Mind in LLMs.

Before CMU, I built multi-agent AI systems at **Adobe** as an Associate Technical Consultant, where I architected pipelines that automated plaintext-to-Fusion conversion and reduced documentation time by 70%. I've also contributed to research at IIT Hyderabad's Spinal Cord Lab and developed computer vision systems for edge deployment at Nosh Robotics.

My work has been accepted at **CoLM 2026**, the **ICML Mechanistic Interpretability Workshop**, and **INTERSPEECH 2026**, with more publications under review at NeurIPS 2026. Beyond research, I love hackathons—I've won first place at events hosted by MIT, HPE, and T-Hub, building everything from pronunciation scorers to yoga pose estimators.

What drives me? The belief that AI should be both **powerful and safe**, capable of augmenting human creativity while remaining interpretable and controllable. Whether I'm probing the hidden layers of foundation models or building real-world agent systems, I'm constantly exploring how we can make AI more capable, more understandable, and more aligned with human values.
